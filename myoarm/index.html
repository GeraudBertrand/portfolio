Galvanism · hardware and signal

MyoArm

A robot arm driven by my muscles. Sensors on my forearm read the electrical activity of the muscles (EMG), a model decodes the intent, and the gripper opens and closes with my grip. ESILV engineering project, due in April 2027.

The idea

The principle of a myoelectric prosthesis. When a muscle contracts, it gives off a small electrical signal that sensors on the skin can read. A model turns that signal into an intention, open, close, how hard, and the gripper of a robot arm follows. In a second phase, motion sensors on the arm and forearm drive the shoulder and the elbow.

Why this one

It is the field I want to work in: neurotechnology, signals from the body turned into control. Prostheses, exoskeletons, human-machine interfaces. This project is my first real step there.

Who does what

We are two. Raphaël takes the robotics and the hardware: the gripper, the mechanics, turning commands into motor movements. I own the whole software chain: acquisition, filtering, decoding, real-time command, safety, and a dashboard that replays recorded sessions.

How it is built

  • A wireless band made by the team: four MyoWare 2.0 EMG sensors on an ESP32-S3, streaming over Wi-Fi to a small PC under Ubuntu.
  • That PC runs the ONNX model and a ROS node that drives an AgileX PiPER arm over its CAN bus.
  • A simple baseline first, LDA or SVM on hand-made features. Deep learning only if it does better.
  • Speed limits and a mandatory emergency stop. A short calibration at each session, because EMG drifts from day to day.

Calendar

  • October 2026: scoping done, sensors and model chosen together.
  • End of November: stable band, clean signal.
  • End of January: gripper and first model.
  • End of February: real-time control.
  • March: tests on the real arm. Mid-April 2027: defence.
