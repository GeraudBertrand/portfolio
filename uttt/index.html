Divination · data and game AI

UTTT

A C++ AI for Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe, written as a pair to beat our teacher's engine. Best result: 72 wins, 17 losses and 11 draws over 100 games against MEDIUM_2.

Where we started

It was the first time we wrote a program that decides on its own. We came in knowing very little game theory or search algorithms, and learned them while building. Seen from there, 72 % against the medium engine is a result I am glad of.

The game

Nine small grids inside a big one. Where you play inside a small grid sends your opponent to the matching small grid. Win three small grids in a row to win the game. Every move is two decisions at once: the square you take, and where you send the other player.

The engine

  • Minimax with alpha-beta pruning and iterative deepening.
  • A transposition table with Zobrist hashing.
  • Move ordering with killer moves and a history table.
  • A positional evaluation, and an opening book that fills itself from tournament games.
  • About 1,600 lines of C++, plugged into the teacher's engine through a fixed interface.

How it got to 72 %

The first version won between 0 and 20 % against MEDIUM_2, at about 350 ms per move. Going to depth 14 with a 10-second budget and adding the opening book brought it to 72 % over 100 games, with an average depth of 11.3 and 319 moves served by the book.

What I learned

  • Samples of 10 games are noise. Only 100-game runs told us anything.
  • Debug and Release builds play like two different engines; always measure with -O3.
  • Mate scores stored in the table must be corrected by distance, or the engine believes nonsense.
  • An opening book goes wrong as soon as the evaluation changes.
  • Not done: quiescence search, multi-threading, Monte Carlo tree search, which is what wins at this game.
